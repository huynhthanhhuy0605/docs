Canva has a built-in **Grids** tool that works well for simple square layouts, but the moment you want a specific number of cells, a curved arc of photos, or a diagonal strip, you run out of options fast. The [Frames Lab](/frames-lab/frames-lab-complete-guide) app builds the collage layout for you — grid, arc, or diagonal — in the exact dimensions you set, then every photo you drop in crops to its cell automatically.

This guide walks through making a photo collage from start to finish, plus three layout styles you can't easily build with Canva's native Grids.

## Make a grid photo collage with Frames Lab

The Grid mode is the fastest route to a classic collage — rows and columns of photos with even spacing.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Frames Lab and pick Grid mode">
From the **Apps** panel in Canva, open Frames Lab, stay on the **Create** tab, and choose **Grid**. This mode builds a collage frame rather than a single-photo frame.
</Step>

<Step title="Set the collage dimensions">
Adjust the grid **width** and **height** so the collage matches your design — a square for Instagram, a tall rectangle for a Story, or a wide banner for a header.
</Step>

<Step title="Choose columns, rows, and gap">
Set the number of **columns** and **rows** for how many photos you want, and the **gap** for the spacing between them. The preview updates live as you change each value.
</Step>

<Step title="Add it to your design and drop in photos">
Click **Add to design**, then drag a photo onto each cell. Each one crops to fit its cell automatically — double-click a photo to reposition it inside.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Fine-tune the grid layout

Two options under the grid controls change the whole look of the collage without starting over.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
<Card title="Wave effect" icon="wave-square">
Open **Wave effect** to curve the collage lines instead of keeping them straight, with controls for the wave's height and speed — a softer, less rigid layout.
</Card>

<Card title="Advanced options" icon="sliders">
Open **Advanced options** to combine cells into larger feature slots and round the corners, so one hero photo can sit alongside smaller ones.
</Card>
</CardGroup>

<Frame caption="Grid wave effect and advanced options — combine cells and round corners">
<img src="/images/frames-lab/frames-lab-grid-advanced-wave.png" alt="Frames Lab grid wave effect controls plus combine cells and corner radius options" />
</Frame>

<Note>
Very complex grids — too many cells or a strong wave — get blocked. If your collage won't add, reduce the rows and columns or lower the wave strength, then try again.
</Note>

## Try an arc or diagonal collage

Grid is only one of the collage shapes. Two more modes give your photos a layout that Canva's native Grids can't produce.

<Steps>
<Step title="Arc — a curved sweep of photos">
Switch to **Arc** mode, set the **sweep angle** (how much of the curve to fill) and the **start angle**, then add it to your design. Your photos fan out along the curve — ideal for a wedding, travel, or celebration collage.
</Step>

<Step title="Diagonal — angled photo strips">
Switch to **Diagonal** mode for strips that run on an angle you control, with the same spacing, gap, wave, and advanced options as Grid. It gives a dynamic, magazine-style collage.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Style the collage

Every frame you add is saved under **Last frames** in the **Style** tab. Select the collage there and add a **border**, **blur**, or **inner shadow** — each with its own color and strength — then re-add it with the effect applied. A thin border between cells is the quickest way to make a busy collage read clearly.

<Tip>
Want a ready-made layout instead of building one? Open the **Explore** tab and click any frame from the themed collections to drop it straight into your design — no setup needed.
</Tip>

## Frames Lab vs Canva's built-in Grids

Canva's native **Grids** (under **Elements → Grids**) are free and fine for a plain square-photo layout. Reach for Frames Lab when you need something Grids can't do: a precise column-and-row count, a curved **arc** of photos, a **diagonal** strip, combined feature cells, or a wave along the edges. Both let you drag a photo in and have it crop automatically — Frames Lab just gives you far more control over the shape of the collage.

## Print or share your collage

Once the collage looks right, download it as **PNG** for sharing online, or **PDF Print** for the cleanest printed result. If you're printing at home, keep the design inside the printable margins.

## Frequently asked questions

<Accordion title="How do I make a photo collage in Canva?">

<Accordion title="Do my photos crop to fit the collage automatically?">
Yes. Drag any photo onto a cell and it crops to that cell's shape automatically. Double-click the photo to reposition or zoom it inside the cell.

The layout is too complex. Reduce the number of rows and columns, or lower the wave strength, then click **Add to design** again.

<Accordion title="Is there a free way to make a collage in Canva?">
Canva's built-in **Grids** (Elements → Grids) are free for simple square layouts. Frames Lab has a free tier too, with a limit on how many frames you can add before upgrading.
